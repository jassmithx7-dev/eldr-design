# Class List

*Refocus for the slice. The yard picks the class. This note says what the four are, and what a fight is. It does not replace [[combat-core]].*

## The list

Four classes. Two or three subclasses each. The names below are the ones to use. Older combat notes still say Thief for Rogue and Caster for Mage. Those are the same classes. No fifth class. No multiclass.

| Class | In a fight | Subclasses |
|-------|------------|------------|
| **Fighter** | Stands where the line needs a body. Hits in melee. Small Eld pool. Spends it to cover someone, or to finish someone already hurt. | Guardian, Champion, Warlord |
| **Rogue** | Moves to the side of the trouble. Makes the company's next hit easier. Small Eld pool. Spends it to get clear, or to show where to hit. | Thief, Assassin, Shadowdancer |
| **Mage** | Casts. Large Eld pool. On a good round, matches a fighter. Does not clear a group alone. | Elemental, Illusion, Warding |
| **Cleric** | Mends, and keeps people standing. The kit for Ravn is only started. | **Paladin**, and two others not named yet |

Paladin is a Cleric subclass. It is not a Fighter subclass. Fighter stays at three: Guardian, Champion, Warlord.

Elemental is the old Evoker name. Warding is the old Abjurer name. Illusion replaces the Controller name. Controller's current buttons are command and fear, so what Illusion actually does is still open.

Arn is the companion who is already a paladin-type. He is in the party for Act 1, then Solen replaces him. Solen is a Devotee, which is the companion word for this kind of work. Her own forks are Priest, Warden, and a third also called Cleric. Ravn's class can be Cleric, and one of his subclasses can be Paladin, without taking Arn's or Solen's name off them. Picking it does not make Ravn a chosen priest. He is a veteran who took that job.

The names on the companions are those people. Bram is a Blade. Fen is a Shadow. Voryn is an Eld-Weaver. None of those names are a class on the table.

Ravn takes one thing off one table. The game says the class in plain words and stops. Hold the Gate comes after the table.

The archetypes are the trees. From level 2, one point per level. Points can go in any of that class's trees. Deepen one, or mix them. That is the World of Warcraft shape already written in [[class-progression]]: four points in the slice can sit 4 in one tree, 2 and 2, or 2 and a dip in each of the others. Deeper buttons in a tree still ask for points already spent in that tree. A capstone still wants 6 in one tree, so the slice does not reach it. Points never cross into another class.

The game suggests a lean. It does not lock it. The first camp is a free respec of those points. The class stays. Companions do not mix. Their kits stay authored.

In the yard, before Hold the Gate, Ravn walks to a table and picks up the thing that names his class. Four tables, no labels. A sword is Fighter. A knife is Rogue. A staff is Mage. A religious textbook is Cleric. The game says the class in plain words and stops. Which faith the book belongs to is not said there.

Level 1 is only the basic hit (or Eld Bolt for a caster). The first real button is the first point. A slice character has 4 points. A capstone takes 6 points in one tree, so it is not in the slice. The cleric's level 1 hit is not named yet.

## The sword first

Tune the Fighter before the other three. Hold the Gate is a line, and the sword is the body that stands in it. Bram is already that body. His default tree is Guardian. A thief makes the company's next hit easier. A caster, on a good round, matches a fighter. A cleric mends. All three of those need the sword's number before they mean anything.

The first fight should be learnable by standing in it. A few buttons, a clear target, pressure that matches a new sword. The knife asks for angles. The staff asks for a spell list. Those come after the swing is a promise.

The target is one Settled Levy ([[enemy-tiers-ashfall]]): 20 HP, Defense 14. The plain sword in the weapon list is the longsword, 1d8 plus Strength ([[combat-core]]). Ravn's Strength is not chosen yet, so this note does not lock his hit chance. The ruler until then is Bram's existing swing: +4 against that Defense, which lands a little more than half the time, and a longsword at Strength 14, which is 1d8+2. Three of those clean hits drop one levy. That is the baseline. Powerful shows up on the finish, not on the first swing.

Execute (Champion) doubles the weapon die only, and only while the target is under half health, on a cooldown. One hurt levy can die on that hit. The pack does not. Guardian takes a hit meant for someone else. Warlord sets up the company. Those three are the Fighter subclasses. Paladin is not one of them. It sits under Cleric. A stun or a jump-the-line stays off the Fighter. On the Rogue, both sit on Shadowdancer only.

## What "powerful" means

Each class should have one moment in a fight where it is the best tool on the field. A little extra strength in that moment is fine. None of them should erase a group on an ordinary turn.

The measure is one Settled Levy: 20 HP, Defense 14. A normal hit takes several clean swings to drop one. The strong moment aims at one person, or at one job, and then it has to wait.

- **Fighter.** Execute rolls the sword's damage die twice, only when that enemy is under half health, and then the button waits. One hurt levy can die. The others are still standing. Guardian's moment is taking a hit meant for someone else. Warlord's moment is making the company's next swing more likely to land.
- **Rogue.** The three moments are below. Setup, one hard cut, or taking someone's turn away.
- **Mage.** On a good round, one spell should hit about as hard as a fighter's swing. It should not delete the group. The two Fireball numbers are still unresolved, so this class is not tuned yet.
- **Cleric.** The moment is keeping someone up. Paladin is named. The other two subclasses, and the numbers, are not written yet.

## The Rogue

The knife is this class. Fen is already in the party. He is a Shadow, which is the companion word for this work, and his usual lean is Assassin. If Ravn takes the knife, Fen is still Fen. They can do the same job.

These three are the Rogue draft. The older tree page still describes movement in tiles. Here, a turn is a few steps and then one action.

Points can mix across the three. In the slice a character has 4 points. A capstone still asks for 6 points in one tree, so Death Mark is part of the class and is not available in the first five levels.

### Thief

This subclass sets up the hit. Someone else in the company does the damage.

**Mark.** You choose one enemy. Their portrait in the turn row gets a mark, so you can see who you set up. The next time anyone in the company hits that enemy, that one hit deals an extra 1d6 damage. That is about 4 extra points of damage. Then the mark is gone. You do not have to be the one who hits them. If nobody hits them, the mark sits for two rounds and then fades. This is the patience. You spend your action pointing at the right person, and someone else collects it.

**Fade.** You step out of sight until you attack, or until your next turn ends.

**Expose.** When an ally's hit connects, you can make that enemy easier to hit for one round. Attacks against them are at +2. This is the same as lowering their Defense by 2.

### Assassin

This subclass takes one soft target. Not the pack. Fen's usual lean is this tree. If Ravn puts points here, it is the same job Fen has, not a bigger one.

A normal knife swing stays a normal knife swing. In the worked example, Fen's hit is 1d6+3. That averages about 6 or 7 damage. A Settled Levy has 20 HP, so one ordinary knife hit does not drop one.

**Exploit.** This is the cooldown hit. It can be used once every three rounds. The hit ignores a shield, so a Settled Levy is attacked against Defense 12 instead of 14. If the target is already under half health, or the target is behind the front line and has no shield, the knife's damage die is rolled twice and the bonus is added once. Fen's 1d6+3 becomes 2d6+3, which averages about 10. A levy who is already at 10 HP often dies to that hit. A back-line archer at 14 HP often dies to it. A fresh shielded levy in front does not. If Exploit hits, that enemy's next attack roll is worse by 2.

**Death Mark.** This is the capstone, and it stays. Once per combat, you choose one enemy. Death Mark itself does no damage. For the rest of the fight, that enemy cannot be healed. If they reach 0 HP, they cannot be stood back up. The company's work on that one person sticks. Your own companions can still be revived. The button asks for 6 points in this tree, so a slice character does not have it yet. It is still the button this subclass is building toward.

### Shadowdancer

This subclass slips past a swing, and can take a turn away.

You step in, cut with a normal knife hit, and step back. If an enemy misses you in melee, you slide away from them. A later button lets you be untargetable for one round. During that round you cannot attack. Vanishing is the whole turn.

**Stun.** Once every three rounds, you freeze one enemy's icon in the turn row. The icon stays there. When their turn comes, it passes, and they do not act. This does no damage. Thief and Assassin do not get it.

**Move up.** Once per combat, you move your own icon to the next place in the row. You act sooner. It is not attached to a basic cut. Thief and Assassin do not get it.

### Side by side, on one levy

A fresh Settled Levy has 20 HP. An ordinary sword or knife hit leaves them standing. The Fighter's Execute is how a hurt one falls. The Thief's Mark adds about 4 damage to one company hit, once. The Assassin's Exploit is a hit near 10 damage on one soft target, and then it cannot be used again for three rounds. Death Mark does not add damage. It makes that one person unable to be healed, and unable to get back up if they fall. The Shadowdancer does not add a big damage number. One enemy loses a turn, or the rogue acts sooner.

## How a fight works

The feel is Chrono Trigger and classic Final Fantasy: the fight is there so the story can keep going, and it should be fun without becoming the whole game. It stays turn-based. You get a few steps, then you act. There is no tile grid and no flanking puzzle.

The order is a fixed list, rolled once. No bars filling up in real time. That clock was set aside on purpose. The fuller on-the-map version, with bars on the body and no separate battle screen, is parked and is not the combat note.

Who is next is a row of icons, in order. It stays on screen. When someone is stunned, their icon stays in the row and reads as frozen. Their turn comes and passes. They do not act. It lasts that one turn unless the ability says longer. Stuns work on enemies and on the party.

Jumping the line is a rare ability, on a cooldown. It moves your icon to next, or pushes someone to the end of this round. It is not part of a basic hit. If every tree can do it, the row stops being a promise. Wait already lets you choose to drop to the end of the round. Controller's Command already takes away someone's main action for a round. A full skip is the stronger version of that, not a second copy on every class.

On a turn, a person can move, do one main thing, and one smaller thing.

- The main thing is a hit, an Eld act, a spell, or helping someone who is down.
- The smaller thing is a potion, extra steps, or one shout when someone nearby goes down.

Three questions, every turn. Where do I stand. What do I spend the turn on. Who do I hit.

The company shows up in two small rules already in [[combat-core]]. If someone else hit that enemy last round, your hit is a little easier. If someone goes down near you, you can shout once to help them stay up. That is the twenty years. It is not a combo meter.

Hold the Gate is also about getting people out. Clearing the enemies is not the only win. That stay locked on the quest.

Eld does not come back during the fight. It comes back at rest. Fighter and Rogue spend a flat pool of 6. The Mage's pool is the large one. Older notes still say Thief and Caster for those two. Which acts you spend it on, cover or finish, is the drift. That happens in the fight. It is not a second choice at the table.

## Checks we keep using

These ride along with every new fight, button, and class moment. They came from the turn-based flavor in Atlas ([[design-doc-turn-based-combat]]). They do not replace the rules above.

After you choose, the next thing you do is choose again. The gap in between is the animation of that choice, not a second showing of the same hit in text, a health bar crawling, or a menu you have to wait out. The icon row stays up so an enemy's turn is something you are reading. Filling bars stay off. A timed button press on a swing stays off. A minigame inside a turn stays off.

Changing the turn row has to change the next decision. A stun or a jump is not extra damage. On the Rogue, only Shadowdancer has them. Fighter, Mage, and Cleric do not get one until a single tree is chosen for that job, and not every tree.

A fight needs a reason besides emptying health bars. Hold the Gate's reason is getting people out. A later fight needs its own second goal, or a new decision in the next few minutes. A bigger number alone is not that reason.

Each class keeps one strong moment, aimed at one person or one job. A little extra strength there is fine. An ordinary turn does not clear the group. The Rogue's moments are already numbered against one Settled Levy. The Mage and the Cleric still owe theirs.

## What this pass leaves alone

The numbers, initiative, morale, and the downed state stay in [[combat-core]]. This note does not retune them.

Still open, and not decided here: the stat array, level-up choices inside a class, the full spell list, knockback numbers, and how a wipe is counted beyond what combat-core already says (back to the last rest, no permadeath, death is a story event).

The trees are already written. This note does not replace them. The number to watch for "powerful, not ridiculous" is Evoker's Fireball: 3d6 to everyone in the blast. A Settled Levy has 20 HP. One of those casts can erase a group. The other Fireball, in the spell list, is 2d6 to one person. Pick one before calling the caster tuned.
