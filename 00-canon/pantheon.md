# Pantheon

*The divine layer of Eldr. Vela and Forn are the primary powers. Six lesser gods operate beneath them — not subordinate, but lesser in scope. Their allegiances are not loyalties: they are interests.*

---

## Vela and Forn

The dominant conflict, thirty years in the making.

**Vela** is the goddess of domination. Patient, incorporating, working through leverage and desire rather than force. Her goal is not conquest in the military sense — it is settlement. A world that has stopped, fully under her will, with nothing passing through that she has not sanctioned. She extends this ambition beyond the mortal world: she intends dominion over the gods themselves. The war is the visible surface of a plan that began three decades ago.

**Forn** is the god of good. Thirty years ago, Vela used something ancient — purchased from Theldorn — to force him into mortal birth and suppress him beneath a real human life: **Arn**. Arn is not Forn-with-amnesia. Arn is a real soul who has never been aware of Forn. Forn is a separate divine presence buried under that life (Johnny/V model — see [[game-premise]]). The friendship is real. Forn cannot be killed by conventional means.

Only Vela and Theldorn know where Forn is. Tyra and Skorra know he is gone — they can feel his absence — but do not know he is bound under a mortal walking in the world. The neutral gods know neither. If any of them knew that Vela had forced a god into mortal suppression, they would be moved by the violation of divine balance. That information is what the story may eventually place in the player's hands.

**When the unmasking completes:** Under mortal stress at the slice climax, Forn breaks through long enough to shatter a Vela-grade soul-trap once. Arn then speaks a final goodbye to the player as Arn. Arn's soul passes to **Lumen**. Forn holds the body — weakened by thirty years of suppression, diminished from what he was. He is not a solution. He is a fragile god who needs protecting, arriving in the place where the player's friend used to be.

**Mordrek note:** Vela's soul-trap at the climax is exactly the kind of theft Mordrek opposes. The beat may later serve as evidence when courting his neutrality crack — do not over-explain it to the player in Act 1.

## The Six Lesser Gods

The lesser gods do not take sides for reasons of love or duty. They take sides — or refuse to — because of what Vela's victory would cost their domain specifically. The path to turning neutrals is not argument from principle. It is showing them exactly what they lose if she wins.

### Aligned with Forn

**Tyra of the Drawn Breath** — Healing, home, community.
The most beloved of the six. Her domain is the fabric of ordinary life — what war erodes and what Vela's dominion would hollow out permanently. She is with Forn not out of theology but because Vela's world has no room for what Tyra is.

**Skorra of the Everwood** — Nature, forest, survival.
With Forn, but on her own terms. She is not anyone's soldier. The Everwood does not take commands. She opposes Vela because dominion requires domestication — a world under one will is a world with its wild places managed, bounded, used. Skorra will not negotiate that.

---

### Neutral — Each Has a Price

**Valdrek the Unbroken** — War, honor.
Opposes Vela philosophically, but not as an ally of Forn. His objection is domain-specific: dominion ends war. A world under Vela's total control is a world without conflict, without the test of arms, without the thing that makes Valdrek's domain real. He will not be commanded by Forn. He needs a specific argument on his own terms — not "help us," but "here is what her victory means for war itself."

**Mordrek of the Final Dark** — Death, passage.
Opposes soul-trapping specifically. His domain is passage — the transit from life to whatever follows. Vela traps souls, bends the dead to her purpose, refuses the passage. That is the one thing Mordrek will not tolerate. On everything else, death comes regardless of who wins. His neutrality has a single crack: show him that she is stealing what is his.

**Morrven of the Deep** — Sea, precise debts.
Runs on contracts. Not loyalty, not philosophy — exact agreements, honored exactly. She will side with whoever makes the better deal and holds to its terms without deviation. The Golden Wake is already courting her. She is the one lesser god whose allegiance can be purchased if the terms are right.

---

### Compromised

**Theldorn the Mindwarden** — Wisdom, secrets, prophecy.
Enthralled with Vela. He wants her. What he sold her — the ancient knowledge that enabled Forn's mortal suppression — was not extracted under duress. He gave it willingly, for desire. The god of wisdom made foolish by wanting the goddess of domination. He cannot act against her not because of obligation but because of what he still feels, and because reckoning with what he chose while feeling it is something he has not yet been able to do. His domain includes prophecy, but this is an attribute of the god, not a narrative device aimed at the protagonist. Nothing Theldorn knows will be handed to the player as destiny.

## Divine Mechanics (Narrative, Not System)

The gods are not resources. They are political actors with interests, debts, and limits. Getting scattered powers to choose to stand together is the shape of the mid-game divine layer — and it works only if each act of alignment costs something real and is earned on the god's own terms.
