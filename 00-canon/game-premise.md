# Game Premise

*This note captures the concrete version of the game direction for the vertical slice.*

## Core Premise

A mature fantasy CRPG about a veteran resistance leader fighting the expanding forces of Vela, a rising goddess of domination. The player has spent years holding the line as Vela's armies push deeper into the world, and the story begins in a world already losing ground.

The player leads a small circle of childhood friends, but the emotional core of the story is the bond with Arn, the player's oldest companion. Arn has been the player's closest friend since birth — they have known each other for thirty years.

### Arn and Forn (Johnny / V model)

Thirty years ago, Vela used something ancient — sold to her by Theldorn — to force **Forn**, the forgotten god of good, into mortal birth and suppress him beneath a real human life. That human is **Arn**.

- **Arn is a real person with a real soul.** The thirty-year friendship is real. He has never been aware of Forn. He does not share Forn's memories, voice, or agenda.
- **Forn is a separate divine presence** buried under that life — not Arn "secretly becoming a god," and not a slow awakening of Arn's gifts.
- The player should be **surprised**. No divine drip. No cutscenes that telegraph Arn's immortality. Combat "nobody really dies" must not be used as foreshadowing.
- When the unmasking completes: **Forn takes the body.** Arn's soul passes to **Lumen**, the world's rest-place. Forn remains — weakened by thirty years of suppression, diminished from what he was — a stranger in the place where the player's friend used to be.
- Forn cannot be killed by conventional means. That fact belongs to Forn / the vessel after the takeover. It is not a hint delivered through Arn surviving wounds earlier.
- Forn is **not a solution and not a party upgrade.** One forced intervention at the climax; then loss.

**The player's stakes are Arn.** The cosmic context — Vela's thirty-year plan, her bid for dominion over gods and world alike — explains why the war is what it is. It is never what the player is fighting to stop. They are fighting for the people in front of them.

## Story Shape

The story opens as a desperate war narrative: **Ashfall Keep** is falling, the resistance is exhausted, and the party must hold the line long enough to protect the living. As the crisis deepens, the player learns that the conflict is older and stranger than it first appeared. The late reveal changes the meaning of the war and the role Arn has always played.

## Vertical-Slice Intent

The first slice should feel focused and contained:

- one front-line stronghold: **Ashfall Keep** (zone focus: the Falling Gate),
- one major questline,
- one major companion relationship,
- and one late revelation that reframes the entire conflict.

## Unmasking Beat (End of Act 1 / Vertical-Slice Climax)

Timing is locked: **end of Act 1**, as the Act 1 boss fight at Ashfall Keep. Arn leaves here; Solen joins at Act 2. The slice must be Arn-heavy so the loss hurts — banter, reliance, shared history — not delayed into Act 2.

Authored sequence — interactive CRPG verbs where possible; not a miracle montage:

1. The **Soulwright** (Act 1 end boss at Ashfall Keep — see [[enemy-roster-ashfall]]) runs a **soul-trap**. **Voryn is present and fighting** the whole beat.
2. **Bram** and **Fen** are **caught** — they read as erased / gone. They are not permanently deleted from the roster; this is a false wipe.
3. Still standing: **player, Arn, and Voryn**. The party appears about to lose completely.
4. The stress on Arn — terror for Bram, Fen, and the people he loves — breaks the suppression. **Forn** surfaces long enough to shatter the soul-trap and the immediate threat **once**. Bram and Fen return shaken.
5. A brief window remains where **Arn is still Arn.** The private beat is one line to the player: "On your left." It is not a Drift-colored conversation. He does not understand that he is (or was bound to) a god. He understands that he is leaving / that something is ending.
6. Arn's soul passes to **Lumen**. **Forn** holds the body. The friend does not come back.

The goodbye does not vary by Drift. There are not three last words. The line is "On your left."

**Outcome lock:** Soulwright at 0 HP before the trap still forces the soul-trap → Arn goodbye → Lumen sequence. Encounter victory is quest-gated, not HP-gated. See [[enemy-roster-ashfall]].

## The Ending

Vela is removed. The balance is not restored by victory — it is restored by replacement.

Forn meets with Voryn privately, without the player knowing. He tells him what must be done. Voryn agrees — eyes open, understanding that he will become evil, necessarily and completely, with no remainder of the person he was. His last act as a person is agreeing to stop being one.

Voryn steps into Vela's domain. The player loses two friends in the same story: Arn when Forn took the body, Voryn when the ending required him.

The party walks out with Dag, Fen, and Bram. Smaller. The world is not saved — it is held. The balance is restored at personal cost and without triumph.

**What keeps Voryn from following Vela's path:** Forn is back (the structural balance exists again), Voryn never wanted what Vela wanted (finite ambition, not totalizing), and something of the mortal Voryn goes with him — not enough to make him good, enough to mean he knows what he holds.

---

## Tone

The game should feel worn, intimate, and desperate. It is not a bright hero fantasy. It is a story about leadership, loss, and the cost of continuing when the world is already falling apart.
