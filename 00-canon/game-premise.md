# Game Premise

*This note captures the concrete version of the game direction for the vertical slice.*

## Core Premise

A mature fantasy CRPG about a veteran resistance leader fighting the expanding forces of Vela, a rising goddess of domination. The player has spent years holding the line as Vela's armies push deeper into the world, and the story begins in a world already losing ground.

The player leads a small circle of childhood friends, but the emotional core of the story is the bond with Arn, the player's oldest companion. Arn has been the player's closest friend since birth — they have known each other for thirty years.

The protagonist's canon name is **Ravn**. He is what becomes now. The present runs through him because he is the one still standing and he acts. He does not become a god. He is not the next coming. No prophecy, no bloodline, no hidden power waking up. Do not define him by the phrase "he is not chosen." The stakes stay the people in front of him.

### Arn and Forn (Johnny / V model)

Thirty years ago, Vela used something ancient — sold to her by Theldorn — to force **Forn**, the forgotten god of good, into mortal birth and suppress him beneath a real human life. That human is **Arn**.

- **Arn is a real person with a real soul.** The thirty-year friendship is real. He has never been aware of Forn. He does not share Forn's memories, voice, or agenda.
- **Forn is a separate divine presence** buried under that life — not Arn "secretly becoming a god," and not a slow awakening of Arn's gifts.
- **Vela wants to rule Eldr.** Forn has beaten her on even terms. She cannot kill him that way. She hid him inside Arn, a real man who does not know, to weaken him. She means to use all the Eld in the world against that weakened god. That blow would kill him. She believes the kill makes her unable to die, like Forn when he is not weakened. She is wrong. They are not the same. If Forn dies, nothing takes his place. If Vela is destroyed, someone must take her place.
- Ordinary people do not know Forn is under Arn. Some blame him for leaving. Some still believe. Some turn to Vela.
- The player should be **surprised**. No divine drip. No cutscenes that telegraph what is under Arn. Do not use Arn surviving a wound as a hint.
- When the unmasking completes: **Forn takes the body.** Arn's soul passes to **Lumen**, the world's rest-place. Forn remains in that body, weakened by thirty years inside Arn — a stranger in the place where the player's friend used to be.
- Forn is **not a solution and not a party upgrade.** One forced intervention at the climax; then loss.

**The player's stakes are the people in front of him.** Vela's thirty-year plan explains why the war is what it is. It is never what the player is fighting to stop.

## Story Shape

The story opens as a desperate war narrative: **Ashfall Keep** is falling, the resistance is exhausted, and the party must hold the line long enough to protect the living. As the crisis deepens, the player learns that the conflict is older and stranger than it first appeared. The late reveal changes the meaning of the war and the role Arn has always played.

## Vertical-Slice Intent

The first slice should feel focused and contained:

- one front-line stronghold: **Ashfall Keep** (zone focus: the Falling Gate),
- one major questline,
- one major companion relationship,
- and one late revelation that reframes the entire conflict.

## Unmasking Beat (End of Act 1 / Vertical-Slice Climax)

Timing is locked: **end of Act 1**, as the Act 1 boss fight at Ashfall Keep. Arn leaves here; Solen joins at Act 2. The slice must be Arn-heavy so the loss hurts. Use reliance and shared history, not a humor routine. Humor between Ravn and Arn is one spare line between equals. Do not delay the loss into Act 2.

Authored sequence — interactive CRPG verbs where possible; not a miracle montage:

1. The **Soulwright** (Act 1 end boss at Ashfall Keep — see [[enemy-roster-ashfall]]) runs a **soul-trap**. **Voryn is present and fighting** the whole beat.
2. **Bram** and **Fen** are **caught** — they read as erased / gone. They are not permanently deleted from the roster; this is a false wipe.
3. Still standing: **player, Arn, and Voryn**. The party appears about to lose completely.
4. The stress on Arn — terror for Bram, Fen, and the people he loves — breaks the suppression. **Forn** surfaces long enough to shatter the soul-trap and the immediate threat **once**. Bram and Fen return shaken.
5. A brief window remains where **Arn is still Arn.** He speaks **one private line** to the player. Voryn may be in the space. The line is Arn to the player. He does not understand that he was bound to a god. He understands that he is leaving.
6. Arn's soul passes to **Lumen**. **Forn** holds the body. Arn is gone.

**Goodbye:** One private line. The old rule — his last words change with the caster's Drift — is replaced by this decision. The line does not change with Drift or class. Do not write the line in this note.

**Act 1 outcome:** Destroying the Soulwright ends that soul-trap. That front is a little better. The war is not over. Arn is still gone.

**Outcome lock:** Soulwright at 0 HP before the trap still forces the soul-trap → Arn's one private line → Lumen sequence. Encounter victory is quest-gated, not HP-gated. See [[enemy-roster-ashfall]]. Do not hint, in Act 1, at anything after the credits.

## The Ending

This is the fact of the ending. It is not a scene, and none of it is hinted in Act 1.

Vela is destroyed. Someone must take her place. Voryn does. He does not come back.

Forn meets with Voryn privately, without the player knowing. He tells him what must be done. Voryn agrees. He knows he will become what she was, with no remainder of the person he was. His last act as a person is agreeing to stop being one. He steps into her place.

She believed that killing Forn would make her unable to die, like Forn when he is not weakened. She was wrong. They are not the same. If Forn dies, nothing takes his place. Forn does not die.

After the credits, Forn leaves Arn's body and ascends. Arn comes back as himself. He was never a god and does not remember being one. This is given, not earned by a clever playthrough. Magic still cannot raise the dead. This return is Forn leaving, not a spell.

Through the played story, Arn is gone from the end of Act 1 on, and Voryn is gone once he takes her place. The party walks out with Dag, Fen, and Bram. Do not hint at Arn's return anywhere in Act 1.

**What keeps Voryn from following Vela's path:** Forn does not die. After the credits he ascends. Voryn never wanted what Vela wanted. Something of the mortal Voryn goes with him — not enough to make him good, enough to mean he knows what he holds.

---

## Tone

The game should feel worn, intimate, and desperate. It is not a bright hero fantasy. It is a story about leadership, loss, and the cost of continuing when the world is already falling apart.
