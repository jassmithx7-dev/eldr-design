# Game Premise

*This note captures the concrete version of the game direction for the vertical slice.*

## Core Premise

A mature fantasy CRPG about a veteran resistance leader fighting the expanding forces of Vela, a rising goddess of domination. The player has spent years holding the line as Vela's armies push deeper into the world, and the story begins in a world already losing ground.

The player leads a small circle of childhood friends, but the emotional core of the story is the bond with Arn, the player's oldest companion. Arn has been the player's closest friend since birth — they have known each other for thirty years.

Arn does not know that he is Forn, the forgotten god of good. He was not suppressed into an existing person — he was born mortal, thirty years ago, as the direct result of a divine act by Vela. She used something ancient, sold to her by Theldorn, to force Forn into a mortal birth. Arn is Forn. The mortal life, the thirty-year friendship, the person the party trusts above all others — all of it is real. The god is the same person.

Forn cannot be killed. Arn cannot be killed by conventional means. This is not apparent to anyone, including Arn.

What changes over the story is not Arn growing into his power but the mask slipping: revelation, not awakening. When it completes, Arn is gone. Forn emerges — weakened by thirty years of suppression, diminished from what he was — and the player's friend does not come back. The player must feel that loss as loss. Forn is not a replacement or an upgrade. He is a stranger who arrived in the place where someone the player loved used to be.

**The player's stakes are Arn.** The cosmic context — Vela's thirty-year plan, her bid for dominion over gods and world alike — explains why the war is what it is. It is never what the player is fighting to stop. They are fighting for the people in front of them.

## Story Shape

The story opens as a desperate war narrative: a frontier outpost is falling, the resistance is exhausted, and the party must hold the line long enough to protect the living. As the crisis deepens, the player learns that the conflict is older and stranger than it first appeared. The late reveal changes the meaning of the war and the role Arn has always played.

## Vertical-Slice Intent

The first slice should feel focused and contained:

- one front-line region,
- one major questline,
- one major companion relationship,
- and one late revelation that reframes the entire conflict.

## Tone

The game should feel worn, intimate, and desperate. It is not a bright hero fantasy. It is a story about leadership, loss, and the cost of continuing when the world is already falling apart.
