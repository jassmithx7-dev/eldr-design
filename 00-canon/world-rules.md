# World Rules

*Single source of truth for what can and cannot happen in Eldr. All fiction must be consistent with this document. Contradictions require a canon-change request, not a silent edit.*

---

## Setting

Three-act structure across three coherent biomes:

- **Act 1** — Mountains
- **Act 2** — Forest
- **Act 3** — Coast

Each act is one biome. No sprawl. Asset discipline is a hard constraint.

---

## Tone & Emotional Register

**Core register:** Friendship in the face of death.

Adults who are aware they are aging, doing dangerous work together. The grimness is tonal and narrative — it lives in the writing, not in punishing mechanics.

**Explicitly rejected:**
- Chosen-one narratives
- Prophecy or bloodline destiny
- Latent power that "awakens"
- Recruit-and-befriend companion loops
- Teenage protagonists
- Saving the world
- Apocalyptic stakes

Stakes are proportionate and human-scale. The protagonist is competent because they worked, not because they were chosen.

## Magic

### The nature of Eld

**Eld** is the in-world term for the ambient magical force. It is present everywhere in the world, uniformly — it does not vary by location, geography, or season.

### Sensitivity

A minority of people are born able to perceive Eld. Most people cannot perceive it at all and never will. This is discovered, not developed — sensitivity is binary, not a spectrum. You either notice Eld or you never will.

Sensitivity is a prerequisite, not a talent. Among those who can perceive Eld, everything after that is the learned trade. A sensitive person who never studies is nothing. A barely-sensitive person who works for twenty years can be formidable.

### Magic as learned trade

Magic is a craft. No innate talent. No bloodlines. No chosen ones. It is studied, practiced, and worked at over years — like any other skilled profession.

**Requirements to study:**
- Eld sensitivity (binary — you either have it or you don't)
- A base level of intelligence. Below a certain threshold, the cognitive load of learning the system is simply not achievable. Some individuals cannot learn magic regardless of sensitivity.
- Years of dedicated study. The opportunity cost is high: time spent learning magic is time not spent developing physical skills. Mages are recognizable by what they are not — they did not spend those years training to fight.

### Racial variation

Some races cannot use magic at all, either due to lacking sensitivity at the population level or due to intelligence ceilings that preclude study. This is a fact about those races, not a punishment — they have developed differently in a world where some races can use magic.

### Stat ceilings

Physical attributes (strength, speed) have racial ceilings. Gender also affects these ceilings: the average male has a higher strength and speed ceiling than the average female. This applies across all races. It is a physical fact about the world, not a social one.

### The bodily cost

Casting uses Eld and takes something physical from the caster that does not fully return. Old practitioners are visibly damaged by their careers. Sensible people do not rely on magic — the cost is real and cumulative.

This is not a resource bar in the narrative sense — it is a fact about what magic does to a body over time. Mechanically it is expressed as Eld (mana), the resource depleted per cast. The long-term bodily degradation is expressed in fiction and NPC characterization.

### What magic cannot do

- Raise the dead.
- Undo a decision.
- Solve a problem the player could have solved by being a better person (no charm, domination, or social bypass).
- Provide reliable information that exploration and investigation are meant to uncover (no divination shortcuts).
- Break geography (no teleportation).
