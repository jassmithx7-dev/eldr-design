# World Rules

*Single source of truth for what can and cannot happen in Eldr. All fiction must be consistent with this document. Contradictions with this document require a canon-change request, not a silent edit.*

---

## Setting

Three-act structure across three coherent biomes:

- **Act 1** — Mountains
- **Act 2** — Forest
- **Act 3** — Coast

Each act is one biome. No sprawl. Asset discipline is a hard constraint.

Setting is pre-technological, low-magic, with a grim and bleak feel. Tone is adult — meaning mature, not gratuitously dark, and explicitly not coming-of-age.

---

## Tone & Emotional Register

**Core register:** Friendship in the face of death.

Adults who are aware they are aging, doing dangerous work together. The grimness is tonal and narrative — it lives in the writing, not in punishing mechanics.

**Explicitly rejected:**
- Chosen-one narratives
- Prophecy or bloodline destiny
- Latent power that "awakens"
- Recruit-and-befriend companion loops
- Teenage protagonists
- Saving the world
- Apocalyptic stakes

Stakes are proportionate and human-scale. The protagonist is competent because they worked, not because they were chosen.

---

## Magic

Magic is a **learned trade**, not a birthright. No innate talent. No bloodlines. It is studied, practiced, and worked at over years — like any craft. This is consistent with the no-chosen-one rule.

Magic has a **bodily cost**. Casting takes something physical from the caster and does not fully return. Old practitioners are visibly damaged by their careers. Sensible people do not rely on it.

This is not a resource bar — it is a fact about the world that explains why powerful magic is rare.

### What magic cannot do

- Raise the dead.
- Undo a decision.
- Solve a problem the player could have solved by being a better person.
