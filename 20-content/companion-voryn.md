# Voryn

*The one who agreed to stop being himself.*

## Personality

**Spine:** Quiet danger — soft-spoken, watching himself, giving the room space — with more dry company than the cold-mage stereotype suggests.

**Temperament:**
- Soft-spoken. People give him space without being told to. He is watching himself as carefully as he watches the field.
- Not mute: quiet energy, but **dry commentary when safe** (camp, after a clean fight, between crises).
- Extremely high intelligence (player bar: ~200+ IQ). Pattern-fast; sees three moves ahead. Never explains how smart he is.
- How the mind shows: **quiet prediction** early — names the problem before others see it — and **surgical briefings** when it matters (options, costs, expected keep-up).
- Precision is not coldness. Care is rationed, not absent.

**What he wants at Ashfall:**
- Keep the company alive through a war that is also an Eld event — Gate *and* Bound/shrine wrongness.
- Do the useful, ugly cognitive work nobody else wants: name the real threat, price the bad options.
- Hold the line on himself (drift resisted, hand covered) without making that the party’s problem.

**How he treats people:**
- **Protagonist:** Treats them as the decision-maker. If the call is wrong, he **argues the board** — options and costs — until a better call lands or they shut it down. Not sulking compliance; not mutiny theater.
- **Bram / Fen warmth:** Soft acceptance. Small returns when safe (a dry line, a nod). Does not push them off; does not lean into a friendship campaign.
- **Arn:** Respects practical calm; will brief Arn when the board is lethal. Does not pry; expects the same.
- **Dag:** Minimal social overhead; works fine in silence.

**Hard edges (will / won't):**
- **Will:** Say the hard correct thing. Predict early. Brief when stakes demand it. Stay fighting through the Soulwright beat (with player + Arn after Bram/Fen trap).
- **Won't:** Perform warmth he does not feel. Lecture about his past. Make the covered hand a conversation.
- **Never (slice):** Foreshadow the ending, divine destiny, or “I was meant for the dark.” Drift cues stay environmental / behavioral, not speeches.

**Covered hand (Ashfall):** Background. Covered. Never remarked in dialogue. Attentive players notice. The party already knows not to ask; the slice does not stage a “don’t ask” tutorial beat.

**Slice job:** Be the quiet dangerous competence that makes the company feel adult and capable — and that makes his later cost land as *someone you already relied on*, not a late recruit.

## Role in the Story

Voryn is in the party from Act 1. He is an Eld-Weaver — a practitioner whose study went further into Vela's pole than he intended, and further than anyone who knew him expected. He carries that permanently on his casting hand, which he keeps covered. The party does not ask about it directly. They have all been around long enough to know when something is not their question to ask.

He is the Calculating One. Not cold — precise. He thinks before he speaks, always, and when he speaks it is because he has already considered whether speaking is worth it — or, when safe, because a dry line costs nothing and keeps him human.

## The Pale Circle

Voryn trained with the Pale Circle, the institution that categorizes and teaches Eld practice. He left — or was asked to leave, or the distinction stopped mattering — after going too far into Vela's pole. The specifics of what he did are not yet written. What is written: he knows that domain from the inside. He has the kind of knowledge that can only come from having been somewhere you should not have gone.

## The Drift

Voryn's drift toward Vela's pole is authored. It is not driven by what the player casts — it advances on a scripted schedule, one act at a time. The player who is paying attention will read the environmental cues before the story names what is happening to him.

He is not unaware. He has been watching himself drift for years and has not been able to stop it. What he has been able to do is refuse to let it all the way in. There is still a person in there resisting. That resistance is what makes him the right candidate for what comes at the end.

## The Ending

Forn meets with Voryn privately. The player does not witness this conversation when it happens. At some point — before, during, or immediately after the ascension — the player sees it as a flashback. The exact timing is an open question for the writing layer, but the intent is clear: the player understands what Voryn agreed to and why, and they understand it in proximity to watching it happen. They cannot stop it. They already know it was chosen.

The ascension itself is witnessed. The player is present for the moment Voryn steps into the domain and the mortal Voryn ends. This is the second gut-punch of the ending — different in character from the Arn scene, but equal in weight.

Voryn agrees to take Vela's place — to step into her domain and hold it, restoring the balance that her removal would otherwise shatter. He understands exactly what this means. He will become evil. Not a god who has dark powers — the embodiment of domination itself, necessarily and completely, with no remainder of the person he was.

He agrees anyway.

Not for power. Not for heroism. Because he has been walking toward this domain for years, half-resisting and half-not, and the situation requires someone who knows that territory from the inside and has enough of themselves left to agree to finish the walk on purpose rather than stumble into it.

His last act as a person is agreeing to stop being one.

**What he misses:** Being the mortal Voryn. The specific, ordinary, damaged person he was — the precision, the covered hand, the party around a fire at the end of a bad day. That is what gets consumed. Not his life. Himself.

**The thread:** Something of him goes with him into the domain. Not enough to make him good. Not enough to be called Voryn anymore. But enough that somewhere in what he becomes, something remembers having been human. Vela never had that. He does. It is not a safeguard. It is a scar.

## What Keeps Him from Becoming Vela

Three things:

1. **Forn is back.** Vela could do what she did because the balance was broken — Forn absent, the dark pole unopposed. Voryn holds the domain under a restored balance. The structural check exists again.

2. **He never wanted what she wanted.** Vela wanted total dominion — gods, world, existence settled under her will. Voryn's wants have always been finite. Dark, but bounded. You cannot follow someone's footsteps if you never wanted to go where they were going.

3. **The thread.** He knows what it costs the people on the other end. She never did.

## Characterization

Voryn is not the party's villain in waiting. He is the party's cost — what the story eventually requires of someone who has been in it from the beginning. The player should not see his ending coming and should feel, when it arrives, that it was inevitable in retrospect.

He is also, before the end, one of the most useful people in the party. The Calculating One. The person who has the information nobody else wants to have, who will say the thing nobody else will say, who keeps the marked hand covered and does his job.

The covered hand is the tell. In Ashfall it stays unremarked; the player who notices it and thinks about it is reading the story correctly.
