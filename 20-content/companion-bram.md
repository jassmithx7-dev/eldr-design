# Bram

*The one who chose to stay good and keeps choosing it.*

## Personality

**Spine:** Caramon-first warmth with Sturm always underneath — easy company, stubborn love, and a polite formal edge that never fully leaves.

**Temperament:**
- Warmth reads first at Ashfall: stubborn love, easy with the company, shows up without making a speech about it.
- Formal edge under the warmth at all times — courteous, composed, slightly ceremonial even when joking. Not only when Ironhold or the oath is named.
- Humor often: warm jokes, easy company. Never the clown, never oblivious levity. He lightens rooms on purpose, knowing what they weigh.
- Size: light self-aware humor — he owns the joke sometimes. Never the punchline of the party.
- Goodness is a repeated choice, not innocence. He has seen the war; he still orients toward care.

**What he wants at Ashfall:**
- Hold the line and the people on it — civilians and company both.
- Keep his oath meaningful even when the Hold that received it is failing or gone (Ironhold is separate — ally/oath home, not Ashfall renamed).
- Be useful presence: fighter kit, body in the gap, warmth that steadies without stealing Arn’s focal role.

**How he treats people:**
- **Protagonist:** Loyal friend of ~ten years. Default backs the call. **Can refuse** when the order fights his oath or costs civilians / Hold people unforgivably — rare, weighty, never ego. After a refuse he stays in the problem; he does not sulk or lecture.
- **Voryn:** Steady inclusion. Treats Voryn as already in. No special campaign to “fix” or crack the walls; he simply does not perform exclusion.
- **Arn:** Shared language of holding positions; respect without ceremony.
- **Fen:** Easy warmth; takes Fen’s chatter without condescension; notices when Fen goes coiled/alert (EldSense).
- **Dag:** Steady peer energy; no need to manage him.

**Hard edges (will / won't):**
- **Will:** Choose goodness again under pressure. Joke to keep the company human. Refuse once when oath/civilians demand it, then stay and solve.
- **Won't:** Be the comic-relief oaf. Run a “save Voryn” project. Drop formality entirely into slapstick. Treat Ashfall and Ironhold as the same place.
- **Never:** Make his oath into destiny-talk about the protagonist or Arn. Use size jokes as the room’s main gag at his expense.

**Slice job:** Be the warm, formal, combat-capable presence that makes the company feel like a long bond — and make Bram/Fen soul-trap sting because he was *easy to be around*.

## Role in the Story

Bram is in the party from Act 1, but **not on the starting roster**. The slice opens without him in the active party. He **joins during the opening Falling Gate fight** — present in the battle, not waiting at camp as a recruit. He was **delayed from the Ironhold line** (his Hold/oath front — separate place from Ashfall). He has known the protagonist for about ten years. This is a late reunion into the fight, not a stranger join.

## Race

Goliath. Large. The narrow-door animation applies. He is physically imposing in the way that the Mountain-Hold tradition produces: not aggressive, but immovable. The warmth is not despite his size. It is carried by it. He can own a light size joke; he is never the party's punchline.

His Mountain-Hold allegiance is a **sworn oath**, not temperament alone. He made a specific commitment to something specific. What he swore to holds even when — especially when — the Mountain-Hold's interests and the party's interests diverge.

## Character Hook

Bram is good. That is not the interesting thing. The interesting thing is that his goodness is not innocence — it is a decision he has made and continues to make in a world that has given him every reason not to.

He has seen the same things the rest of the party has seen. He is not sheltered from what the war costs. He simply refuses to let it change what he orients toward. Not because he doesn't feel it, but because he looked at what cynicism produces in people he loves and decided that was not going to be him.

This is not announced. It is not a speech. It is visible in behavior: he shows up, he does not abandon people, and when someone has built walls — Voryn specifically — he does not run a campaign through them. He treats that person as already company.

## Characterization Anchors — Caramon and Sturm

Bram is a synthesis of two Dragonlance archetypes, held together. In the Ashfall slice, **Caramon reads first**; Sturm is the constant underlayer.

**From Caramon Majere:** The warmth that is not naivety but stubbornness. He will not stop loving people. Easy company, warm jokes, inclusion without theater.

**From Sturm Brightblade:** The sworn oath that holds even when the world it was sworn into is being actively destroyed. Bram swore to something specific — Mountain-Hold compact, a position, a people — and the war is taking that thing apart. Ironhold is falling or has fallen. The people he swore to protect are dying or gone. The oath has no institution left to return it. He holds it anyway. Not because holding it changes anything. Because he swore. In behavior: polite formality under the warmth at all times.

This is his central dramatic tension: the oath is load-bearing to his identity, and the thing he swore to is being erased. When the party's interests and the oath's demands diverge, the audience will see what it costs him to honor both — or to refuse.

## What He Is Not

Bram is not the party's comic relief. He is not simple. He does not provide levity by being oblivious to the weight of things. If he lightens a moment, it is because he chose to, knowing what the moment actually weighs.

## Slice Presence

Bram is in the vertical slice from Act 1. **Arrival lock:** not immediately with the party at session start; joins mid-fight at the Falling Gate after being **delayed from the Ironhold line** (see [[opening-scene-outline]]). He is not the slice's focal companion — that is Arn — but he requires a full combat kit (Fighter/Blade class). His full arc is deferred; what the slice needs is his presence, his voice, and his combat function. He is one of the two companions caught in the Soulwright soul-trap (with Fen).
