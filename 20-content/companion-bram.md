# Bram

*The one who chose to stay good and keeps choosing it.*

## Role in the Story

Bram joins the party in Acts 2–3. He has known the protagonist for approximately twenty years. He is not new — he is someone who has been present in the background of the party's history and steps into the active story when the story requires what he is.

## Race

Goliath. Large. The narrow-door animation applies. He is physically imposing in the way that the Mountain-Hold tradition produces: not aggressive, but immovable. The warmth is not despite his size. It is carried by it.

His Mountain-Hold allegiance is a **sworn oath**, not temperament alone. He made a specific commitment to something specific. What he swore to holds even when — especially when — the Mountain-Hold's interests and the party's interests diverge.

## Character Hook

Bram is good. That is not the interesting thing. The interesting thing is that his goodness is not innocence — it is a decision he has made and continues to make in a world that has given him every reason not to.

He has seen the same things the rest of the party has seen. He is not sheltered from what the war costs. He simply refuses to let it change what he orients toward. Not because he doesn't feel it, but because he looked at what cynicism produces in people he loves and decided that was not going to be him.

This is not announced. It is not a speech. It is visible in behavior: he shows up, he does not abandon people, and when he encounters someone who has built walls — Voryn specifically — he does not push through the walls. He simply does not acknowledge that they are meant to keep him out.

## Characterization Anchors — Caramon and Sturm

Bram is a synthesis of two Dragonlance archetypes, held together.

**From Caramon Majere:** The warmth that is not naivety but stubbornness. He will not stop loving people. He reaches people who have built walls not by pushing through them but by declining to acknowledge they were meant to keep him out. The warmth that Voryn cannot deflect is not warmth that ignores what Voryn is — it is warmth that has decided Voryn is worth it anyway, without saying so.

**From Sturm Brightblade:** The sworn oath that holds even when the world it was sworn into is being actively destroyed. Bram swore to something specific — Mountain-Hold compact, a position, a people — and the war is taking that thing apart. Ironhold is falling or has fallen. The people he swore to protect are dying or gone. The oath has no institution left to return it. He holds it anyway. Not because holding it changes anything. Because he swore.

This is his central dramatic tension: the oath is load-bearing to his identity, and the thing he swore to is being erased. When the party's interests and the oath's demands diverge, the audience will see what it costs him to honor both — or to choose.

## What He Is Not

Bram is not the party's comic relief. He is not simple. He does not provide levity by being oblivious to the weight of things. If he lightens a moment, it is because he chose to, knowing what the moment actually weighs.

## Slice Presence

Bram is not in the vertical slice. He is established here as a foundation for Acts 2–3. No further design work on him until the slice is complete.
