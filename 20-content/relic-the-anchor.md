# The Anchor

*The mechanism of Forn's suppression. Not loot. Not a puzzle. Backstory made physical.*

---

## Physical Form

A sphere the size of a fist, dense and dark — not glass, not stone, not clearly either. Not reflective. Something shifts inside when it catches light but never resolves into a shape. There are no markings, no seams, no apparent opening. It does not look made. The crack running across its surface was not there when Vela's servants placed it; it has been spreading.

---

## What It Is

The Anchor is a sealed vessel holding **Forn's** severed divine self-knowledge — his memory of being a god, extracted at the moment of forced mortal suppression thirty years ago. Without it, Forn would have broken through his mortal binding decades before the events of the slice.

The Anchor is the lock. **Arn's** life is the cage.

**Theldorn** provided the mechanism. He knew how gods choose to incarnate willingly — a sacred act of divine self-giving. He gave **Vela** its inversion: a forced version, with the god's self-knowledge extracted and sealed externally rather than carried into the mortal life. The transfer was willing on Theldorn's part. He has not reckoned with what he chose. He is not monitoring the Anchor — he gave it to Vela thirty years ago and has not tracked what became of it. Enthrallment, not conspiracy. He does not know it is failing.

---

## Location

Buried in the shrine beneath **Ashfall Keep**. Placed here by Vela's servants when the suppression was first enacted thirty years ago. It is not concealed — it is maintained. The shrine exists to house and feed it.

---

## Maintenance

The **Soulwright's** soul-trap ritual is not opportunistic harvesting. It is upkeep. Trapped souls feed the Anchor, sustaining the seal against Forn's buried presence. The Soulwright is the Anchor's warden; every soul taken beneath the keep has gone toward keeping the lock intact.

This has been ongoing. The shrine beneath the gate is not new.

---

## Why It Is Failing

The siege of Ashfall Keep has disrupted the maintenance ritual. Regular soul-feeding has been impossible for weeks before the player descends. The Anchor has been starving.

By the time the party reaches the shrine, the suppression is already cracking from attrition — not from anything the player, Arn, or the party has done. The siege is the cause. Arn's emotional stress at the climax tips a suppression that is already failing, not one that is intact.

---

## Role in the Climax

The degraded Anchor explains why Forn is closest to the surface *here*, in the shrine, at this moment. Proximity to the starved vessel is proximity to the thinning seal.

When Arn's stress peaks — Bram and Fen soul-trapped — Forn breaks through the remaining suppression and shatters the Soulwright's trap. The Anchor shatters simultaneously; Forn's emergence consumes it. No artifact survives. Nothing is retrievable.

Full beat order: [[game-premise]]. Boss staging: [[enemy-roster-ashfall]].

---

## Player Agency

None. The Anchor is not findable as an object, not interactable, not usable. The player may learn it existed — the shrine is evidence of its maintenance — but they cannot stop its degradation, accelerate it, or intervene in its shattering. It is backstory that explains the mechanics of the climax.

---

## Mordrek Thread

Every soul the Soulwright trapped fed the Anchor rather than passing to Mordrek's domain. Thirty years of such theft — the shrine beneath the gate is a crime scene. This is the evidence that will eventually bear on Mordrek's neutrality in Act 2.

Do not telegraph this to the player in Act 1. The shrine's function is not explained in dialogue during the slice.
