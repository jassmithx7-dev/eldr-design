# Arn

*The player's oldest friend and the emotional anchor of the story.*

## Personality

**Spine:** The person who has always been there — and who treats that as work, not poetry.

**Temperament:**
- Calm under pressure; does not perform calm. He reduces the temperature of a room by being practical.
- Short sentences when things are bad. Slightly longer when the party is safe and tired.
- Humor is one spare line between equals, not a routine. Never the clown, never oblivious. If he lightens something, he knows what it weighs.
- Watches the others. Notices when Fen is coiled, when Bram is holding an oath too hard, when Voryn has gone quiet. Does not always comment.
- Physical presence is steady, not large-drama. He stands where the line needs a body.

**What he wants at Ashfall:**
- Keep people alive through the Falling Gate — civilians first, then the company.
- Hold the protagonist together without making it a speech. He has done this for thirty years.
- Get through the next hour. He is not thinking about gods, destiny, or meaning. He is thinking about the wall, the wounded, and who is still outside.

**How he treats people:**
- **Protagonist (Ravn):** Ravn leads, as Aragorn and Tanis do. Arn stands beside him as a peer, as Legolas and Sturm do, because Arn chooses to. They can disagree. Arn stays and still helps. Oldest friend — shorthand, unfinished sentences, trust that does not need proving every scene. He will say the hard practical thing. He will not theatricalize love. After a disagreement he does not gloat.
- **Bram:** Respect without ceremony. They share the language of holding positions.
- **Fen:** Protective without smothering; trusts Fen's read of people; sometimes translates Fen's loyalty into orders the rest can use. At Dag's fire, Fen came inside. When Arn means Fen is wound up, he means Fen is wound up. He does not mean Fen is still standing on the gate.
- **Voryn:** Does not pry about the hand or the Pale Circle. Treats him as part of the company. Quiet check-ins, not interrogation.
- **Dag:** Easy old familiarity; Arn is one of the people who can read Dag's armor.

**Hard edges (will / won't):**
- **Will:** Put himself in the gap so someone else doesn't have to. Argue for evacuation over glory. Stay when leaving would be smarter for him alone. Refuse the protagonist when the alternative is abandoning people who cannot fight — then still try to fix it together.
- **Won't:** Perform hope he does not feel. Claim destiny, prophecy, or specialness for anyone — especially himself. Make the protagonist's grief about Arn's feelings. Refuse over ego, pride, or "I know better" theater.
- **Never (writer rule):** Mystical self-awareness, holy metaphors about his own body, "I was meant for this," or uncanny survival talk. He does not know Forn exists.

**Slice job:** Make the player feel the friendship as load-bearing *before* the Soulwright beat — through reliance and history, not foreshadowing.

## Role in the Story

Arn is the player's oldest companion, someone who has been present since before the war. He is calm, measured, and deeply trusted. He is the one person the player has always been able to rely on. He stands beside Ravn because he chooses to.

## Character Function

Arn serves three purposes at once:

- **Emotional anchor:** He grounds the player and gives the party a sense of history.
- **Moral counterweight:** He is steady in the face of fear and does not easily abandon people.
- **Mythic hinge:** He is the person most tied to the deeper truth of the story, even though he does not know it.

## Hidden Truth

Forn, the forgotten god of good, is suppressed beneath Arn's life. Arn is a real soul and has never been aware of Forn (Johnny/V model — see [[game-premise]]). Do not drip divine hints. The player should be surprised at the unmasking beat.

## Class

Arn is a **paladin-type**: a fighter with a secondary healing function. Designer truth: the healing is leakage from suppressed Forn, not Eld. Player-facing: he is simply the party's steadier healer — never framed as uncanny, miraculous, or "he should be dead." No survival tells.

He is the party's healer in Act 1. When he leaves at the end of Act 1 and Solen joins in Act 2, Solen covers the healing role through different means.

## Eld and Magic

Arn is outside the Eld system entirely. He does not have Eld sensitivity in any conventional sense, and he does not cast. His suppressed divinity is not Eld-based — it operates by different rules that are not the player's to observe or measure. This should never be called out mechanically. It is simply true that Arn does not cast, and the party does not find this remarkable.

## Party Tenure

Arn is in the party from Act 1. At the **end of Act 1** boss, the unmasking beat plays (Bram/Fen soul-trap; Voryn still fighting; Forn breaks the trap once → Arn's one private line to the player → Arn's soul passes; Forn holds the body). The old rule that his last words change with the caster's Drift is replaced by this decision. Solen joins at the start of Act 2. The gap between losing Arn and gaining Solen should be felt. Act 1 content must make Arn load-bearing so the loss hurts. Do not hint, in Act 1, at anything after the credits.

## Story Use

Arn should remain present through the entire slice. The player should feel his importance as a friend long before the reveal — through loyalty and history, not through mysterious power tells.
