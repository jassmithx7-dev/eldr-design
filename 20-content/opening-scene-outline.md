# Opening Scene Outline

*The first scene of the vertical slice.*

## Purpose

The opening should establish three things immediately:

1. The world is already losing ground.
2. The player is a tired, experienced leader.
3. The party is a group of old friends carrying history into a war they did not start.

## Scene Summary

The player is already in the Falling Gate courtyard, mid-retreat. The resistance is already falling back, covering the final evacuation of **Varnstead**. The outer walls are damaged, the yard crowded with frightened civilians. The party's job is not to win — it is to hold long enough for the retreat not to collapse into panic. The first thing heard is the yard, below. Steel has not started.

## Key Beats

1. **The world is already in retreat.**  
   The player sees smoke, broken barricades, and people carrying what little they can save.

2. **The player is recognized as the one who holds the line.**  
   People look to them for orders, not because they are glamorous, but because they have survived long enough to be trusted.

3. **Arn is present as the steady companion.**  
   He does not speak like a hero. He speaks like someone who has been there before and knows what it costs.

4. **Bram joins mid-fight — not on the starting roster.**  
   He was **delayed from the Ironhold line** (oath/Hold front — not Ashfall renamed). He arrives into the Gate battle as a reunion (~20-year bond), not a stranger recruit. Warmth + formal edge; no long reunion speech mid-fight. One beat can name Ironhold delay without a lore dump.

5. **The first sign of the supernatural appears.**  
   A shrine or chapel inside the gatehouse is contaminated by strange shadow or residue. The danger is no longer just soldiers and sieges. **Fen EldSense fires here** (pose + “Wait.” / “Something’s wrong.” + portrait pulse) — first teach of the alarm. He does not explain Eld.

6. **The scene ends with urgency, not clarity.**  
   The gate holds for now, but the party realizes that the enemy is not merely advancing; it is changing the world itself.

## From the title to the yard

The title is the word Eldr and a way to begin. Beginning does not open a character sheet. It puts Ravn in the courtyard, and the yard plays. Arn, Fen, and Voryn are with him. Bram is not there. Dag is not there.

Ravn is his name. The player does not choose it.

When the yard's last line is done, and before any steel, Ravn gets his gear. Three tables stand in different parts of the courtyard. No labels. A sword on one, a knife on one, a staff on one.

> **Arn:** "Ravn. Your gear is in the yard. Go get it."

He walks to a table and takes what is on it. The game then says the class in plain words. A sword: "That's a fighter." A knife: "That's a thief." A staff: "That's a caster." It stops there. There is no subclass list yet. Then Hold the Gate. This does not happen after the fight.

## How a conversation is built

Write what people do before you write what they say. Each person wants one thing from the talk. A line asks for that, or answers an ask, in ordinary words. The talk ends when someone does the thing. They sound different because they want different things. Fen asks for the people outside. Voryn names what waiting costs. Arn names the person in front of him. Ravn gives the order. No slogan. No line that means something other than what it says.

## The yard (draft)

*Before any steel. The first scene. They disagree, Ravn calls it, they stay. Bram is not here. Dag is not here. No dialogue menu. Speech is plain. People say what they mean.*

Smoke. Broken timber. **Varnstead** is still coming through the gate with whatever they could carry. The last knot of them is still outside. Fen is talking, because the quiet makes this worse. Those people are already his.

> **Fen:** "Ravn. They're still outside. Give them a moment."

> **Voryn:** "If we wait, we close the gate on the people already coming through."

Arn is with the person nearest him who cannot run. He does not take a side. He says what that person needs, and he looks at Ravn.

> **Arn:** "They can't run. Get them on the cart."

<!-- ASSUMPTION: Ravn sends the cart through, then the gate closes. He does not leave it open. The call is authored. The player is not offered a menu. -->

> **Ravn:** "Get the cart through the gate. Fen, go with it. Then we close the gate."

Fen shuts up and moves. Voryn stays. He is already watching the gate. Arn goes back to the person on the ground.

> **Arn:** "Bram is still on the Ironhold line."

> **Voryn:** "Dag is still packing the gear. He's not in the yard."

Nothing else about either of them. You do not hear how anyone met.

The yard can be walked while this is still true. A cart. A bundle someone turned around for. A face looks at Ravn, because he has survived long enough to be the one who answers.

> **Ravn:** "Go through the gate. Stay with the cart."

That is the last line. No steel. The hold has not started.

## Tone

The opening should feel exhausted, practical, and haunted. It should not feel like a triumphant beginning. It should feel like the last time anyone still believes they can hold something together.
