# Protagonist

*The person the player builds. The Captain the party already knows.*

---

## What Is Fixed

These are not player choices. They are the character before the character creation screen.

- **Human.** Not negotiable. The story turns on a thirty-year friendship with Arn — a human man born the same generation. The protagonist's humanity is load-bearing.
- **Male.** Fixed. The only female in the party is Solen.
- **Age: 30s or older.** The player does not choose age. The protagonist has been doing this work long enough that the competence is legible and the fatigue is real. He is not young.
- **Veteran resistance leader.** He has led this company for approximately twenty years. He did not inherit the role. He earned it through the kind of repeated decisions that leave marks. The party calls him Captain.
- **What he is.** Ravn is what becomes now. The present runs through him because he is the one still standing and he acts. He does not become a god. The stakes stay the people in front of him. His significance is the friendship with Arn, the place he holds in the resistance, and the fact that he acts.
- **He is not the next coming.** Do not define him by the phrase "he is not chosen."
- **Still rejected:** prophecy, bloodline, and a hidden power waking up. Those plots stay out.

---

## What the Player Builds

These are the character creation decisions. Classic CRPG structure.

- **Name** — player-editable. Canon default for internal reference: **Ravn**. The player's chosen name is used in all shipped content. Voiced dialogue from companions uses the title "Captain" — this is how people address him in the world, and it allows VO to remain name-agnostic without sounding evasive.
- **Class** — three choices: Fighter, Thief, or Caster. See [[combat-core]] and [[eld-magic]] for full mechanical detail. The class is fixed at creation — the protagonist's competence is pre-established and specific, not a generalist background that could have gone any direction.
- **Stats** — distributed from standard array at creation. Class determines HP dice and attack bonus progression.
- **Build** — within class, the player makes choices at level-up. What those choices are depends on the class-progression note (not yet written).

## The Three Classes — What They Mean for the Story

All three classes experience Drift. The Drift Counter, the thresholds, and the environmental cues apply regardless of class. The class changes what form the declaration takes.

Arn's goodbye does not change with class or Drift. The old rule that his last words change with the caster's Drift is replaced by this decision. The goodbye is one private line. It is the same line for every class. Do not write the line here.

The world is saturated with Eld. Everyone has some sensitivity to it. Fighters and Thieves cannot cast spells, but they can channel Eld into a small set of class-specific combat abilities. Each ability is pole-flavored — using it moves the Drift Counter. See [[combat-core]] for the full ability lists.

### Fighter

The Captain who held the line through physical discipline and tactical competence. Twenty years of leading dangerous people through dangerous situations. He has earned his position by being the most effective person in a fight and knowing how to put others in the right places.

His Eld pool is small (6, flat). His abilities are not spells — they are instinctive Eld use, the kind that a veteran develops without studying it. Holding a position so an ally doesn't take the hit (Forn). Finishing a wounded enemy before they can recover (Vela). Every use is a declaration.

### Thief

The Captain who survived twenty years by knowing how to move unseen and how to read a room before anyone else. The resistance has needed this as much as it has needed fighters.

His Eld pool is small (6, flat). His abilities are about positioning and exploitation — disappearing from an enemy's attention (Forn) or finding the crack in someone's guard (Vela). Fen's Eld sensitivity creates a specific dynamic: two people who move through the same dark spaces, one of whom feels the Eld consciously and one of whom uses it without quite naming it.

### Caster

The Eld practitioner. The Captain who studied and paid the physical cost for it. His casting hand may show it.

His pool is large and scales with INT and level. He has access to all nine schools and the full spell list. He also experiences Drift most immediately — every school he reaches for is a choice about what he is becoming, and the choices come faster and more consequentially than they do for a Fighter or Thief. His Drift runs alongside Voryn's authored descent. The ending lands with particular weight when the player has been making the same kind of decisions the whole way through.

## The Friendship with Arn

Ravn leads, as Aragorn and Tanis do. Arn stands beside him as a peer, as Legolas and Sturm do, because Arn chooses to. They can disagree. Arn stays and still helps. Humor is one spare line between equals, not a routine. Do not write the scene lines in this note.

The protagonist has known Arn since birth. Thirty years. The friendship predates the war, predates the company, predates every decision either of them has made as an adult.

This is the emotional spine of the game and it is **not player-authored.** The player cannot choose how the protagonist feels about Arn. The relationship is pre-established and load-bearing. Where a line exists, it may be warmer, drier, or more direct. It does not change the bond.

When Forn takes the body and Arn's soul passes to **Lumen**, the protagonist loses Arn for the rest of the played story. The player cannot opt out of that loss. It is authored. What happens after the credits is in [[game-premise]]. Do not hint at it in Act 1.

## Class-Agnostic Writing Rule

All dialogue, quest text, and companion reactions must be written to work for any protagonist class. References to specific combat actions (swinging a sword, casting a spell, picking a lock) are acceptable in written descriptions; they are not acceptable in dialogue from companions who would not know which class the protagonist chose.

The exception: if a companion *would* know — because they were present and saw him act — they can reference what they saw. "That was a hell of a spell" from Bram after a fight is fine. "You always were the best caster I knew" as a general statement of character is not.

## What He Is Not

- Not young. Not finding himself. The story is not about growing up.
- Not silent. This is not a voiced blank-slate protagonist. His perspective is present in how the player-chosen dialogue options are written — they have a register, an intelligence, a history.
- Not exceptional by birth. Everything he is, he made.
- Not the center of the divine conflict. The cosmic scope is context. The player's felt stakes are the people in front of him.
