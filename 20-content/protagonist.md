# Protagonist

*The person the player builds. The Captain the party already knows.*

---

## What Is Fixed

These are not player choices. They are the character before the character creation screen.

- **Human.** Not negotiable. The story turns on a thirty-year friendship with Arn — a human man born the same generation. The protagonist's humanity is load-bearing.
- **Male.** Fixed. The only female in the party is Solen.
- **Age: 30s or older.** The player does not choose age. The protagonist has been doing this work long enough that the competence is legible and the fatigue is real. He is not young.
- **Veteran resistance leader.** He has led this company for approximately twenty years. He did not inherit the role. He earned it through the kind of repeated decisions that leave marks. The party calls him Captain.
- **No bloodline. No prophecy. No chosen-one.** His significance to the story is the friendship with Arn and the position he holds in the resistance. Not origin. Not destiny.

---

## What the Player Builds

These are the character creation decisions. Classic CRPG structure.

- **Name** — player-editable. The game uses this name in text throughout. A canon default exists for internal reference ("The Captain") but the player's chosen name is used in all shipped content. Voiced dialogue from companions uses the title "Captain" — this is how people address him in the world, and it allows VO to remain name-agnostic without sounding evasive.
- **Class** — three choices: Fighter, Thief, or Caster. See [[combat-core]] and [[eld-magic]] for full mechanical detail. The class is fixed at creation — the protagonist's competence is pre-established and specific, not a generalist background that could have gone any direction.
- **Stats** — distributed from standard array at creation. Class determines HP dice and attack bonus progression.
- **Build** — within class, the player makes choices at level-up. What those choices are depends on the class-progression note (not yet written).

## The Three Classes — What They Mean for the Story

All three classes are valid. The story works with any of them. But they produce different relationships to the game's systems.

### Fighter

The combat specialist. The Captain who held the line through physical discipline and tactical competence. He has earned his position by being the most dangerous person in a fight and knowing how to put other dangerous people in the right places.

He does not use Eld. He watches the Drift mechanic play out in Voryn from the outside — aware that something is happening, unable to measure it, reading the world's cues alongside the player.

### Thief

The shadow operator. The Captain who survived twenty years by knowing how to move unseen and how to read a room before anyone else. The resistance has needed this as much as it has needed fighters.

He does not use Eld, but the Illusion school's out-of-combat tools (Minor Illusion, Blur) overlap with what a Thief does naturally. Fen's Eld sensitivity creates a specific dynamic — two people who move through the same dark spaces, one of whom feels the Eld and one of whom doesn't.

### Caster

The Eld practitioner. The Captain who studied and paid the physical cost for it. His casting hand may show it, depending on how long he has been at this.

This is the richest protagonist experience for the Drift mechanic. A Caster protagonist does not just observe what is happening to Voryn — he experiences the same pressure from a different starting position. Every school he reaches for is a choice about what he is becoming, running in parallel with Voryn's authored descent. The ending lands differently when the player has been making the same kind of decisions the whole way through.

## The Friendship with Arn

The protagonist has known Arn since birth. Thirty years. The friendship predates the war, predates the company, predates every decision either of them has made as an adult.

This is the emotional spine of the game and it is **not player-authored.** The player cannot choose how the protagonist feels about Arn. The relationship is pre-established and load-bearing. Dialogue options, where they exist, vary in register — warmer, drier, more direct — but they do not vary in the fundamental fact of the bond.

When Arn is displaced and Forn emerges, the protagonist loses his oldest friend. The game does not let the player opt out of that loss. It is authored.

---

## Class-Agnostic Writing Rule

All dialogue, quest text, and companion reactions must be written to work for any protagonist class. References to specific combat actions (swinging a sword, casting a spell, picking a lock) are acceptable in written descriptions; they are not acceptable in dialogue from companions who would not know which class the protagonist chose.

The exception: if a companion *would* know — because they were present and saw him act — they can reference what they saw. "That was a hell of a spell" from Bram after a fight is fine. "You always were the best caster I knew" as a general statement of character is not.

---

## What He Is Not

- Not young. Not finding himself. The story is not about growing up.
- Not silent. This is not a voiced blank-slate protagonist. His perspective is present in how the player-chosen dialogue options are written — they have a register, an intelligence, a history.
- Not exceptional by birth. Everything he is, he made.
- Not the center of the divine conflict. The cosmic scope is context. The player's felt stakes are the people in front of him.
