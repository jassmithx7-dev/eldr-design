# Solen

*The one who still believes, arriving in the ruins of everyone else's faith.*

## Role in the Story

Solen joins the party at the start of Act 2, immediately after the Arn/Forn displacement. She is a Devotee — a practitioner of prayer-based healing in the Tyra tradition. She takes the field role Arn left. She cannot fill what Arn was. She does not try.

She is the only woman in the party. She is also the only stranger — everyone else has twenty years of shared history. The gap is real and nobody pretends otherwise. What closes it, if it closes, happens through Acts 2 and 3.

## Class

**Devotee (Healer).** Her healing is prayer-based — she petitions Tyra, goddess of healing and community, and the request is answered. Her class kit is centered on restoration and support: healing, stabilization, and minor protective blessings. She does not carry weapons for offense; she carries them because the war requires it.

Her healing operates through divine channel rather than Eld manipulation — a different mechanism from the player caster's Healing school, though the combat outcomes are similar. She does not have Eld sensitivity.

## Faith

Solen's faith in Tyra is genuine and intact. It is not innocence — she has been a healer in a losing war long enough to know what it costs and what it cannot fix. She has patched people together who died anyway. She has prayed over people she could not save. The faith survived it. Not because she has not looked at the cost squarely, but because she looked at it squarely and kept believing anyway.

She arrives into a party that has just had its relationship to faith destroyed.

Arn — the person they trusted above anyone, the one whose steadiness held them together — turned out to be a suppressed god, and the man is gone. What is left is Forn, a stranger wearing someone they loved. The party is standing in the wreckage of what it means to believe in a person and a world that turns out to have been stranger and colder than they understood.

Solen arrives believing. She does not announce it. She does not push it on anyone. But it is visible — in how she prays before she sleeps, in the way she speaks about Tyra without embarrassment, in the certainty that sits underneath her practicality. It is a reminder, to everyone around her, of something they do not have anymore.

This is not comfortable for the party. It is not meant to be.

## Character Hook

She is not soft. Healers who survive a war this long are not soft. She is direct, sometimes blunt, with a dry register that comes from spending years saying true things to people who needed to hear them and didn't want to.

She is also the most functional person in the party at the start of Act 2, because she did not lose Arn. She arrived after. She is grieving the war, not the man. That functional difference creates distance she cannot close through effort — only through time and shared experience.

What the party gives her, eventually, is the thing a healer who has worked alone does not have: people who stay. She has been with companies that broke up, relocated, disbanded. This party has been together for twenty years. Whatever she thinks of them in the first weeks, she can see what that permanence looks like, and she has not had it.

## Rhyme with Bram

Solen and Bram are the party's two people who have chosen to keep orienting toward something good in a world working against it. His is an oath to a place and a people being erased by the war. Hers is a faith in a god whose domain — home, healing, community — is exactly what the war destroys.

They are not the same. Bram's goodness is chosen stubbornly in the face of evidence. Solen's is grounded in something she believes is true about the world. But they recognize each other. They are the two people in the party least likely to spiral.

## What She Is Not

Solen is not a replacement for Arn. She is not a consolation. She does not arrive to make the Arn-shaped absence easier. She is a different person doing a different version of the same job, and the party will feel the difference every time.

She is not the party's spiritual counselor. Her faith is hers. She does not proselytize. If someone asks her about Tyra, she will answer honestly. Nobody is required to ask.

## Slice Presence

Solen is not in the vertical slice. The slice ends at the Arn/Forn displacement — she arrives in the beat immediately after. This note establishes foundation for Acts 2–3. No further design work on her until the slice is complete.
