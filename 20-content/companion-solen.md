# Solen

*The one whose faith was real, and answered by the wrong god.*

## Role in the Story

Solen joins the party at the start of Act 2, immediately after the Arn/Forn displacement. She is a Devotee — a practitioner of prayer-based healing, trained in the Tyra tradition. She takes the field role Arn left. She cannot fill what Arn was. She does not try.

She is the only woman in the party. She is also the only person in it who arrived as a stranger — everyone else has twenty years of shared history. The gap is real and nobody pretends otherwise. What closes it, if it closes, happens through the story of Acts 2 and 3.

## Class

**Devotee (Healer).** Her healing is prayer-based — she petitions Tyra, goddess of healing and community, and the request is answered. Her class kit is centered on restoration and support: Cure Wounds and Lesser Restoration variants, stabilization effects, and minor protective blessings. She does not carry weapons for offense; she carries them because the war requires it.

She does not have Eld sensitivity. Her healing operates through divine channel, not Eld manipulation — a different mechanism from the player caster's Healing school, though the combat outcomes are similar. The distinction matters to the pantheon. It does not show up in her stat block.

## The Vela Problem

Tyra's power has been diminished for thirty years. Forn is suppressed; without him, the balance is broken, and the Forn-aligned gods feel the absence. Tyra can still act, but her reach is reduced — her answered prayers arrive weaker, slower, less consistently.

Vela noticed the gap.

For some period of time — it is not established how long — Vela has been intercepting select prayers addressed to Tyra and answering them herself. Not all of them. Enough that a faithful healer in a high-value position would not notice the difference: the prayers worked, the wounded recovered, the faith held.

Solen's prayers have been among those intercepted. She does not know this.

**Why Vela does it:** She needs the party alive. Whatever Vela's plan requires of the protagonist's company — and the full shape of that is a story question, not answered here — she has an interest in keeping them operational. A healer in the party who does not know she is a Vela instrument is more useful than a healer who does.

**What this costs Solen when she discovers it:** Everything she oriented by. Not just her faith — her past. Every person she saved, every prayer she believed was heard by a god who cared what happened to the wounded and the frightened: she has to hold the possibility that some of those saves were Vela keeping a resource operational. She cannot know which ones. She cannot go back.

## The Discovery

Solen figures it out herself. No NPC tells her. No divine revelation arrives. She notices something — a detail that does not add up, a prayer answered in a way Tyra's tradition does not account for, a moment of clarity about the shape of what has been happening to her faith — and she follows it to the conclusion.

The specific mechanism of the discovery is an open question for the writing layer. The constraint is that she arrives at it through intelligence and attention, not through being told. The god of wisdom is compromised; no one has that information to give her. She has to be smart enough to see it herself.

## Character Hook

Solen is not naive. She has been a healer in a losing war long enough to know what it costs and what it cannot fix. Her faith is not innocence — it is a decision she has kept making in a world that has given her every reason to stop.

This is where she rhymes with Bram: both are people who have chosen to keep orienting toward something good in a world actively working against it. The difference is that Bram's oath is to a place and a people and a tradition. Solen's is to a god. And the god, it turns out, was not who she thought.

She is not shattered by the discovery. She is a person who has watched people die and kept working. She will not fall apart. But something in her that was load-bearing has been taken out, and she is standing on what's left of it, and nobody in the party except possibly Bram will know how to respond to that without making it worse.

## What She Is Not

Solen is not a replacement for Arn. She is not a consolation. She is not an easier version of the grief the party is carrying about Arn's displacement. She arrives in a hole and does not pretend the hole is not there.

She is also not the party's moral center. Arn was that — his steadiness, his calm, the weight of his presence. Solen is a pragmatist whose pragmatism has spiritual roots. Those are different things, and the party will feel the difference.

## Relationship with Voryn

Of all the party members, Voryn is the one she watches most carefully. A practitioner who has been walking toward Vela's pole for years — she recognizes something in the shape of that, now that she knows what Vela's reach feels like from the inside. She does not speak about it directly. Neither does he. The acknowledgment, if it ever comes, is a scene for the writing layer.

## Slice Presence

Solen is not in the vertical slice. The slice ends at the Arn/Forn displacement — she arrives in the beat immediately after. Her note establishes foundation for Acts 2–3. No further design work on her until the slice is complete.
